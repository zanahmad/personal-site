\section*{From image geometry to a tracer balance --- review sheet}
Use this after attempting the worked examples and practice. Explain each claim in words before calculating.

\section*{Key ideas}
\begin{itemize}
\item Image, mask, mesh and solution are different objects.
\item Boundary conditions and wall assumptions affect the calculated flow.
\item The classroom washout model is a single compartment, not CFD.
\item Numerical checks and independent validation answer different questions.
\end{itemize}
\section*{Common errors and repairs}
\textbf{An attractive mesh proves the geometry is correct.} Inspect source-image agreement, physical scale, connectivity and boundary labels.

\textbf{V/Q is the exact departure time of each particle.} It is the time constant of the ideal well-mixed concentration curve.

\textbf{A washout curve predicts stroke.} It is a model output; outcome claims need separate biological and validation evidence.

\section*{Retrieve without your notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item What remains when Q=\allowbreak{}0?
\item What happens to the curve when Q and V both double?
\item What would make the small pocket behave differently here?
\end{itemize}
\section*{Readiness check}
\begin{itemize}
\item Distinguish an intensity image, a segmentation, a mesh and a flow solution.
\item Specify the missing boundary and wall assumptions for a synthetic chamber.
\item Interpret a well-mixed washout comparison without calling it spatial CFD.
\item Separate a numerical check from validation against independent observations.
\end{itemize}
