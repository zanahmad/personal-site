\section*{Probability calibration and missing-modality evaluation --- review sheet}
Use this after attempting the worked examples and practice. Explain each claim in words before calculating.

\section*{Key ideas}
\begin{itemize}
\item Ranking, calibration and probability scores answer different questions.
\item Calibration parameters are fitted on separate validation groups.
\item A positive temperature preserves ranking and threshold-0.5 decisions.
\item Evaluate modalities on the same cases and freeze the missing-data rule.
\item Missingness stress tests require a stated intervention and limited claim.
\end{itemize}
\section*{Common errors and repairs}
\textbf{Call a lower Brier score proof of better calibration alone.} Inspect reliability summaries and their counts; Brier also reflects discrimination.

\textbf{Refit the imputer when test B values disappear.} Use the stored training median and all fitted parameters unchanged.

\textbf{Expect AB with no B to equal a separately fitted A model.} The models have different coefficients and calibration parameters.

\section*{Retrieve without your notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item What happens at temperature one?
\item What does a zero-count reliability bin tell us?
\item What should remain fixed during the missingness test?
\end{itemize}
\section*{Readiness check}
\begin{itemize}
\item Distinguish ranking discrimination, probability quality and calibration.
\item Fit a single temperature using validation groups while keeping test groups untouched.
\item Compare one- and two-modality models on the identical held-out group population.
\item Evaluate a frozen missing-data rule and state what a synthetic stress test can support.
\end{itemize}
