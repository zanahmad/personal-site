\section*{Implicit pressure equations and ideal-valve complementarity --- review sheet}
Use this after attempting the worked examples and practice. Explain each claim in words before calculating.

\section*{Key ideas}
\begin{itemize}
\item Differentiate or discretize stored CP when compliance varies.
\item An active link adds a symmetric conductance block to the pressure matrix.
\item A fixed valve guess creates a linear solve; consistency remains to be checked.
\item Conservation alone cannot select the correct valve state.
\item An independent constant-compliance reference reveals time error.
\end{itemize}
\section*{Common errors and repairs}
\textbf{Replacing the volume derivative with C times pressure derivative while C changes.} Retain P times compliance derivative, or directly difference CP.

\textbf{Clipping valve flow after solving once.} Change the valve state and solve its new coupled balance.

\textbf{Calling a conserved implicit result exact.} Compare the same problem under refinement and with an independent reference.

\section*{Retrieve without your notes}
\begin{itemize}
\item What units must h/R have in the pressure matrix?
\item What happens to the active conductance terms when all compartment balances are summed?
\item Can both open and closed labels describe the same state at zero pressure difference?
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}

\item Check conservation, valve complementarity, units and time-step sensitivity separately.
\item Implement a two-compartment matrix in a guided scaffold and connect it to the six-compartment method.
\end{itemize}
